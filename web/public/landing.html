[story]

Built from an audit, aimed at the axis nobody claimed.

01

It started with an audit

A clean-sheet review of a full open agent OS found five criticals: an unauthenticated execution seam, red lines that failed open, a model approving its own actions. Guardrails bolted onto an agent loop fail. The gate chain had to be the architecture.

02

The market split left an axis empty

2026's agent market splits into camps — provider SDKs racing on capability, frameworks on reach, MCP at ~97M downloads a month — while policy is everywhere advisory, shaped by vendors, and unenforceable. Accountability stayed second-class.

03

The bet

Invert the product. Capability is borrowed — your model, your tools, any MCP mount. Accountability is built — deny-by-default, human-only approval, an append-only ledger. The result is the agent you can let act unattended, because the record answers for it.

[features]

Structural, not supervised.

Every framework sells guardrails as a feature. Here they are the architecture.

[gate] governance is the architecture
Every call walks registry → trust ceiling → policy engine → human approval. No member exits the chain — spawning a sub-agent is itself a gated call.
[policy] policy is external and yours
An open wire spec to whatever policy engine you run. Engine errors escalate — never execute. Swap the policy server without touching the agent.
[approver] a model is never the approver
Approval is always human, with reasons, blast radius, the delegation chain, and rollback hints in the copy.
[ledger] provenance and privacy are products
What acted, on what, with whose approval — with PII stripped, counts never values, command text never stored, denials recorded immediately.
[growth] self-improvement, gated
Skills are proposed, metered, and adopted through the same gate as everything else — then checked for drift and retired.
[posture] a binary, not a platform
Rust, Apache-2.0, one binary. MCP-native in both directions — serves tools, mounts others'.

wins here

  • Unattended work — the agent stops for you, never around you: approval reaches your phone.
  • Multi-tenant and regulated contexts — ceilings per tenant, session tags, a ledger an auditor can read.
  • Policy that must bind — external, enforceable, swappable; engine errors escalate, never execute.
  • The adoption curve — audit mode lets you watch verdicts before they bind.

not the right tool here

  • Raw capability breadth — it rides your model and your MCP tools; it adds no secret capability of its own.
  • Frictionless autonomy — every consequential call waits for a human. That is the product.
  • Platform features — no hosted cloud, no vendor tool marketplace.
